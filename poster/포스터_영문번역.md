## Introduction

- Wearable elbow assistive devices for home-based rehabilitation after discharge should provide adequate assistance while minimizing wearing burden, including donning/doffing time, fixation, pressure, weight, and size.
- Conventional rigid exoskeletons require the mechanical joint axis to be precisely aligned with the human elbow axis, and their weight and structural complexity may increase wearing burden. Devices that are cumbersome, uncomfortable, or heavy are difficult to use consistently at home.
- Therefore, this study aimed to design a frame-free elbow flexion assistive device using flexible bands and a twisted string actuator (TSA), considering the average anthropometric dimensions of Korean males in their twenties and motor performance, and to evaluate its wearability.

## Method

### 1. Anthropometry-Based Simulation

- Average values for males aged 20–24 from the 8th Size Korea survey (upper arm circumference, elbow circumference, arm length, upper arm length, axilla height, elbow height, and body weight) were used [1].

**Table. Size Korea 8th survey, average values for males aged 20–24 [1]**

| Measurement | Mean | Applied in simulator |
|---|---|---|
| Upper arm circumference (straight) | 341.1 mm | Upper arm radius 5.43 cm |
| Elbow circumference (straight) | 262.6 mm | Forearm radius 4.18 cm |
| Arm length | 590.6 mm | Forearm length 25.7 cm (arm length − upper arm length) |
| Upper arm length | 333.4 mm | Upper arm length 33.3 cm |
| Axilla height | 1296.5 mm | Elbow-to-axilla distance 23.6 cm (axilla height − elbow height) |
| Elbow height (flexed) | 1060.7 mm | – |
| Body weight | 72.7 kg | Forearm and hand mass (2.52 % of body weight) |

- A JavaScript simulator was developed to calculate string contraction length, tension, motor torque, and number of revolutions according to band position and flexion angle.
- Calculations assumed 50 % assistance of the forearm weight with the upper arm hanging vertically. The forearm-and-hand mass ratio (2.52 % of body weight) and center-of-mass location were taken from Plagenhoef et al. [2].
- Band positions on the upper arm and forearm and the hub diameter were determined from the calculation results.

### 2. Band Pattern and Hardware

- Based on the 3D human body shape of Korean males in their twenties from the 6th Size Korea survey, band patterns fitted to the curved arm surface were developed using Geomagic Design X and YUKA CAD [1].
- Neoprene fabric was cut according to the patterns and the edges were stitched to produce bands 5 cm (upper arm) and 3 cm (forearm) wide, fastened with hook-and-loop (Velcro).
- A GL30 motor, an ESP32-C3 SuperMini, and a SimpleFOC driver were mounted on the upper arm band, and the upper arm and forearm bands were connected by PE braided line. A shoulder strap was used to prevent slippage. Parts connecting the bands, PE line, and motor were printed on a Bambu Lab P1S.
- IMU sensors (MPU-6050) were attached to the upper arm and forearm bands.
- The total device mass was 173.5 g (excluding battery).

### 3. Principle and Control

- When two strands of PE braided line are twisted by the motor, the distance between the two ends shortens as the strands form a helix, pulling the elbow into flexion [3].

  (Untwisted length)² = (Twisted length)² + (r·θ)²
  r: distance from rotation axis to string center (2 mm), θ: twist angle of the motor

- The elbow flexion angle was calculated in real time from two IMUs.
- A smartphone was connected via Wi-Fi to control the device in manual mode (speed control) and automatic mode (string length adjusted to follow flexion angle), with the arm posture displayed in 3D.
- Flexion up to approximately 100° (the joint limit of the mannequin arm) was confirmed on a mannequin arm.

## Results

### 1. Design Results (Simulation)

- Placing the forearm band farther from the elbow decreased the required tension but increased the contraction length. Placing the upper arm band farther from the elbow lengthened the string and reduced the required contraction ratio.
- Accordingly, the upper arm band was placed as far from the elbow as possible within the elbow-to-axilla distance (mean 23.6 cm), and the forearm band was placed 6–9 cm from the elbow.
- The contraction length and tension required for 60° flexion were calculated with the forearm attachment at 9 cm.
- A smaller distance between the rotation axis and the string center increases the required number of revolutions, whereas a larger distance increases the required torque. A target distance of 2 mm, at which both values remained within 50 % of the motor rating, was selected, and the hub diameter was determined accordingly.

**Table. Requirements for 60° flexion**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Contraction length | 8.3 cm |
| Tension | 14 N |
| Hub diameter | ≥ 4 mm |

### 2. Wearability Evaluation

- Ten males in their twenties wore the unpowered device and performed repeated elbow flexion–extension, after which wearability was rated on 12 items using a 5-point scale.

**Table. Wearability evaluation results**

| Domain | Items | Mean ± SD |
|---|---|---|
| Donning | Donning/doffing time, band length | 4.10 ± 0.97 |
| Fixation | Upper arm band, forearm band, shoulder strap | 4.30 ± 0.88 |
| Pressure and irritation | Tightness pain, pressing/rubbing, numbness | 4.00 ± 1.14 |
| Weight and motion | Weight burden, motion interference | 4.50 ± 0.61 |
| Acceptance | Device size, overall satisfaction | 4.15 ± 0.75 |
| Total | 12 items | 4.20 ± 0.91 |

- All domains showed mean scores of 4.0 or higher (total 4.20 ± 0.91), indicating positive wearing satisfaction. Weight and motion (4.50 ± 0.61) scored highest, while pressure and irritation (4.00 ± 1.14) scored relatively lowest.

---

## Conclusion

- Based on the average anthropometric dimensions of Korean males in their twenties, a band placement criterion was proposed: the upper arm band as far from the elbow as possible below the axilla, and the forearm band 6–9 cm from the elbow.
- Based on the contraction length (8.3 cm) and tension (14 N) required for 60° flexion, a hub diameter of at least 4 mm was selected, and a 173.5 g prototype consisting of flexible bands and a TSA without a rigid frame was fabricated.
- In the wearability evaluation, all domains showed mean scores of 4.0 or higher (total 4.20 ± 0.91).
- → A design basis for wearable elbow assistive devices integrating anthropometric data, structural design, and wearability evaluation was presented.

## Future Work

- Since an external power supply was used and the wearability evaluation was conducted without power, wearing comfort and assistive effects during actuation will be evaluated after integrating a battery into the upper arm band.
- Since the device was designed and evaluated based on the average dimensions of Korean males in their twenties, the evaluation will be extended to females, older adults, and actual rehabilitation patients to verify the applicability of the band pattern and placement criteria.
